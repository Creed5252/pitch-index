The Pitch Index · Methodology v1.0

How The Pitch Index works

Every number on the rankings page comes from public match results and one published formula. This page explains it at three depths: the one-minute version, the full explanation, and expandable deep dives with the exact math, parameters and code.

The one-minute version
  1. We collect every scored game in each elite league, plus the showcase, qualifier and cross-league events those teams play in.
  2. Each game tells us one thing: how much better one team was than the other that day, measured by goal margin, capped at 6.
  3. We solve for the single set of team ratings that best explains every result at once. Beating a strong team moves you up more than beating a weak one. That is strength of schedule, built in.
  4. Rankings refresh every Tuesday morning. Every game, every rating, and every past week's table is published on GitHub so anyone can check our work.

1. What the rating means

A team's rating is the number of goals it would be expected to beat an average team in its own table by, on a neutral field. A rating of +2.0 means "about two goals better than the typical team in this league and age group." Ratings are comparable within one table (for example ECNL Girls U15) and not across tables.

The rank is simply the order of ratings. The SoS column is the average rating of the opponents a team has actually played.

3. How each game is weighted

4. Strength of schedule

Strength of schedule is not a bolt-on bonus. It is how the rating is solved. Every game becomes an equation: Team A's rating minus Team B's rating should equal the margin. We solve all of those equations together, so a team's rating depends on how good its opponents turned out to be, and their ratings depend on their opponents, all the way across the league.

In practice, a team's rating is very close to:

rating  ≈  average capped margin  +  average opponent rating (SoS)

So a team that wins by two every week against strong opponents rates higher than a team that wins by three against weak ones.

5. Home field

For league games we estimate one home-edge number per table from the results themselves (it is usually 0.1 to 0.3 goals). The home team's result is adjusted by that amount before it counts. Showcase, qualifier and tournament games are treated as neutral sites.

6. Showcases and national qualifiers

Most league seasons are played inside conferences, which means a Southwest team may never play a Southeast team in league play. Showcases, national events and qualifiers are where those teams meet. Those games act as bridges: they let the formula compare conferences that never play each other directly. More bridges mean a more trustworthy national ranking.

Some events (for example the EA Winter Qualifier) have not been played yet for older age groups. Rankings still publish for those ages; they will shift once those games are in. That movement is expected.

7. Who gets ranked, and when a table is "national"

Every team with at least one scored game is ranked; there is no minimum. Early in the season, and for teams that have played only a few games, the rank range is wider and the spot will move more as results come in. A table is published as one national list when at least 80% of its teams are linked together through games, directly or through common opponents. When a table is less connected than that (often early in the season, or in leagues that play only inside regions), we publish it by conference or region instead of pretending we can compare teams that have no games in common. Every league is published as one national list (ECNL, ECNL RL, EA, EA2, GA, DPL and N1L). Their conferences and divisions already rank teams locally, so a conference-only table adds nothing. EA, GA and DPL also offer West region and East region views, and N1L a conference filter: the same national ratings, filtered and numbered within the selection. N1L Premier 1 and Premier 2 are separate lists and are never ranked against each other. When conferences have not yet met at a national event, we place each unconnected conference on the national scale using its clubs: a club whose other teams in the league (other age groups, linked through national events) rate well lifts its conference. Teams placed this way are marked est., because the placement is an estimate, not a result. If a conference has fewer than 3 teams with a club link, it is marked no link yet. As national events are played, real results replace the estimate.

8. Ties and early-season tiers

Every table is ranked in tiers, so a ranking exists from the first weekend, whether or not national events have been played.

  1. Rating, read in bands of 0.10 goals. Teams in the same band are treated as tied.
  2. Goal difference per game breaks the tie.
  3. Goals for per game, then fewest goals against per game.
  4. More games played, then team name.

Early in the season most comparisons across conferences are estimates (est.), so these result-based tiers do more of the work. As national events connect the conferences, the rating does more and the tiers matter less. We still compute a rank range from six alternate runs and publish it in the data files for anyone checking our work; it is no longer shown on the rankings page.

9. Team name cleanup

The same team often appears under slightly different names across schedules ("Bay Area Surf - ECNL G10" vs "Bay Area Surf ECNL G10", extra spaces, birth-year formats). Before rating, every name passes through a cleanup step: spacing and separators are normalized, and known aliases are mapped to one canonical team. Every alias we merge is listed publicly in teams/aliases.csv on GitHub. If you see a split or wrong merge, tell us and we fix it in the next weekly run.

12. Known limits

  • Results only. We do not use rosters, player movement, injuries or scouting.
  • Early in the season, conference-only play limits how well regions can be compared. Teams placed by club link are marked est. until national events connect them.
  • Some leagues post scores late. A missing score is a missing game until the league posts it.
  • Cross-league games (for example ECNL vs MLS NEXT) are not used in v1.
  • N1L is the hardest league to rank nationally. It is organized far more regionally than the other elite leagues: districts play only inside themselves and there are no national qualifying events in the regular season. Its national lists lean on each team’s results and schedule inside its own district, so treat them as a rough national picture, most useful within a conference.

13. Deep dives

For anyone who wants to reproduce the numbers.

The exact math formula

For a table with n teams and m games, unknowns are the ratings r1…rn and a home edge h. Each game k between home team i and away team j gives one row:

r_i − r_j + h·[league game]  ≈  clip(goals_i − goals_j, −4, +4)

We solve the ridge-regularized least-squares problem:

minimize  Σ_k ( r_i − r_j + h·home_k − y_k )²  +  λ · Σ_t r_t²
λ = 1.0   (home edge h is not shrunk)

which has the closed-form solution b = (AᵀA + λI)⁻¹ Aᵀy, where each row of A has +1 for the home team, −1 for the away team and 1 in the home column for league games.

SoS for a team is the mean of its opponents' ratings over the games it played (counting repeat opponents each time).

How connectivity is checked graph

Treat every team as a dot and every game as a line between two dots. Teams that can reach each other through any chain of games form a connected group. If the largest group holds at least 80% of the table's teams, the table is ranked nationally (teams outside the main group are flagged). Otherwise each group is ranked on its own and labeled with its conference or region. We publish the group count and main-group share for every table every week.

How the rank range is computed stability

Six alternate runs, each changing one setting: cap 5, cap 8, λ 0.5, λ 2.0, event weight 0.75, event weight 1.5. For each team we record the best and worst rank across the base run plus those six. Nothing is averaged; the range is the honest min and max.

Team name cleanup rules identity
  1. Trim and collapse whitespace; normalize separators (" - " becomes a space).
  2. Automatic merge: names that differ only in capitalization, spacing, punctuation, or how birth years are written (2013/14, 2013/2014, 2013-2014, 13/14) are the same team. The most-used spelling is kept. Words are never dropped, so "X Academy" and "X" stay separate teams. Every automatic merge is listed in that week's tables.json under name_merges.
  3. Manual alias table for real renames and sponsor-name changes, published as teams/aliases.csv with the date and reason for each merge.
  4. Different age-group squads of the same club are never merged.
Reproduce it yourself open source

The GitHub repository holds the rating code, the parameters file, the alias table, and a folder for every weekly run containing the full game ledger (CSV) and the published tables (JSON and CSV). To reproduce a week:

git clone https://github.com/Creed5252/pitch-index
cd pitch-index
pip install numpy
python model/rate.py --week 2026-12-08

The output should match the published tables exactly. If it does not, that is a bug and we want to hear about it.
